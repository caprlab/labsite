## 1. Overview & Position Description

### Welcome to the CAPR Lab
As a Research Assistant (RA) in the Cognitive, Applied, and Perceptual Research (CAPR) Lab, you serve as an official representative of Dr. Bonny, the Department of Psychology, and Morgan State University. This role carries important academic and scientific responsibilities, and we have certain expectations of you. Failure to meet the guidelines outlined in this handbook could result in you being dismissed from the lab.

### Position Description
* **Title:** Undergraduate Research Assistant (RA)
* **Type:** Volunteer / Unpaid Position
* **Primary Role:** Assist in conducting research on memory development, managing study participants, entering/coding behavioral data, participating in scholarly meetings, engaging in professional development, and maintaining lab operations.

> **The Nature of Volunteer Roles:**  
> Although this is an unpaid/volunteer position, you must treat this as a professional position, similar to what you would if you were working in a business office. We rely heavily on your commitment, accuracy, and punctuality to run scientific studies.

#### Key Responsibilities
* **Time Commitment:** Commit to a minimum of **4 hours per week**. This hourly requirement includes time spent running participants, processing data, attending lab meetings, and completing assigned scholarly readings. Note that the specific minimum hours may be higher if placement is for course credit.
* **Participant Interaction:** Welcome, guide, and test study participants courteously and professionally.
* **Data Processing:** Perform data collection, behavioral coding, and accurate data entry supporting research projects.
* **Scholarly Reading:** Read assigned research journal articles and book chapters to build foundational knowledge and adequately prepare for team discussions. 
* **Lab Meetings & Active Participation:** Attend all scheduled lab meetings and actively contribute to team discussions, project reviews, and lab presentations.
* **Skill Development & Technical Growth:** Actively engage in building technical research skills, including learning programming languages and software for experiment building (e.g., jsPsych) or data analysis and visualization (e.g., R, Python).
* **Research Ethics & Compliance Training:** Complete all required university research ethics training modules prior to data collection, including CITI Program training.
* **Lab Maintenance & Security:** Follow strict physical security and digital data-privacy protocols.

## 2. Onboarding & Offboarding Checklists

To maintain organized lab operations, all RAs must complete specific administrative tasks when joining and leaving the lab.

### Onboarding Requirements
* **CITI Training:** Complete required research ethics training and submit certificates to lab leadership.
* **JATOS Account:** Request a JATOS account on `psyresearch.morgan.edu` for experiment management.
* **System Access:** Request access to the lab's Google Drive folders, shared lab inbox, and Slack workspace.
* **Website Profile:** Submit a professional photo and a brief RA profile bio for the official CAPR Lab website.

### Offboarding Requirements
* **Removing Access:** Ensure your access to the shared inbox, Slack, and Google Drive is properly sunsetted.
* **Handoff:** Document where you left off on tasks and ensure all code and files are pushed to the shared Google Drive.

## 3. Attendance, Punctuality & Time Tracking

### Attendance Expectations
* **Punctuality:** Arrive on time for your scheduled in-person hours, remote work, and lab meetings. We count on your being here during your scheduled hours, so please adhere to them.
* **Absences & Tardiness:** Please give at least **24 hours notice** if you will be late or need to miss your scheduled in-person hours or meetings, and arrange for make-up hours as soon as possible. 
* **Unacceptable Excuses:** Missing your hours because you need to study for another class is **not** an acceptable reason to miss lab. You should not regularly miss your scheduled hours, doing so could jeopardize your position in the lab.
* **Vacation Planning:** If you plan on taking vacation time outside of the normal Thanksgiving, fall, Spring, etc. breaks, let us know as soon as you have plans set.

### Time Tracking
* **Daily Timesheets:** It is your responsibility to keep correct documentation of your hours working on lab tasks in-person and remotely on your official lab time sheet.
* **Task Logging:** This helps us keep track of your efforts as well as how long tasks are taking to complete.

## 4. Software Systems, Data Management & Open Science

### Digital Platforms
* **Morgan State Email & Shared Inbox:** Important lab information, such as participant appointments and lab meeting readings, will be sent to your Morgan State email address. Please read, and if necessary respond to, these emails.
* **Slack:** Used for day-to-day team updates, check-ins, quick task questions, and immediate announcements. Keep notification settings active during working hours.
* **Sona Systems:** Manage participant recruitment, study scheduling, and crediting. Cross-check appointment times before shifts and report no-shows promptly.
* **Google Workspace:** Project files and documentation are managed via our institutional Google Drive (Google Shop).

### Data Management & Storage Requirements
It is critical to distinguish between general research data (which is OK to share within the lab) and confidential participant data.
* **Secure Storage:** All Personally Identifiable Information (PII) and confidential data from participants (e.g., names, phone numbers, signatures, audiovisual recordings) must be stored securely either in locked lab cabinets or in lab-provided, password-protected Google Drive folders. 
* **Local Devices:** You must **never** save participant PII or confidential data to local personal devices (e.g., your personal laptop or flash drive). 

### Open Science Documentation
Transparency and reproducibility are vital. When writing code (e.g., R, Python, jsPsych) or creating data entry protocols, keeping code and documentation up-to-date and clear for others to read is mandatory. Use standard file naming conventions and comment your scripts thoroughly.

### Strict NO AI Policy for Participant PII
Under no circumstances should you input participant PII or confidential participant data (e.g., names, contact info, recordings) into generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude). Doing so is a strict violation of data privacy protocols.

## 5. Security, Ethics & Data Confidentiality (PII Protocols)

### Protecting Personally Identifiable Information (PII)
Our lab makes a promise to keep ALL personally identifiable information (PII; e.g., first and last name, date of birth) absolutely confidential, and as a member of the lab, it is your duty to never breach this confidentiality.
* **Identification:** When testing, identify participants by first name and last initial only. Use particiant IDs for marking data.
* **Document Handling:** Never throw away anything with participant information on it (shred it instead).
* **Digital Data Security:** Lock computers when you’re not using them. **Never** upload or email files containing PII. 
* **Physical Data Protection:** Never remove data or anything with participant information from the lab.

### Professional Conduct in the Lab
* **Attire:** When you are running participants, consider what you are wearing before you come to the lab; if you think that your outfit could distract the participants from the study (and mess up data collection), consider wearing something else.
* **Phones:** Personal phone calls not allowed in the lab. Please put your phone in silent mode when you enter the lab.
* **Noise:** Manage voice volume in the lab (especially when testing is going on). You should be mindful of the studies that can be going on.
* **Food & Drink:** Make sure that you do not bring food or drink into the lab. Participants can be sensitive to food smells and eating sounds, which can interfere with testing.
* **Equipment:** Secure lab equipment at the end of your shift.

### Participant Communication Ethics
* Please be courteous to participants: they are taking time out of their day to complete a study. Always be positive when speaking to participants.
* We cannot stress enough that you must not compare participants or make evaluative statements (e.g., 'you did a lot better than the last person'), especially if they can be taken the wrong way.
* Do not talk about participants at the lab or outside of the lab because this reflects poorly on us and will impact the way people see us in the community.

### Physical Security
* **Door Policy:** Keep all doors in the lab closed and locked when rooms are not in use. 
* **Unattended Spaces:** If you leave the lab unattended at any time, please ensure the door is locked! Even if you are coming right back or just going to get a drink of water, etc., close the door (it will lock automatically).
* **Personal Belongings:** Please be conscientious with security and cautious with your personal belongings.

## 6. Work Quality, Authorship & Feedback

### Quality Control
The quality of your work is very important to the research process and we rely heavily on your commitment to the lab. Your work is the basis of all of our coding schemes and the data is used in the analyses that lead us to our conclusions about cognition. 
* Always prioritize accuracy over speed. Please try your best.
* Do not be afraid to ask questions if you are unsure. We are here to help!

### Open Communication & Feedback
We want your experience in the lab to be an enjoyable one and for you to learn a lot. 
* **Issues/Challenges:** If you are having any difficulties, please be sure to talk to someone in the lab (one of the RAs, the grad students, or Dr. Bonny).
* **Ideas & Observations:** We are also very interested in making good use of your intellectual resources, so we would love to hear your observations, ideas, and questions about our work.

### Authorship vs. Acknowledgements
* **Acknowledgements:** Routine RA duties (e.g., running participants, data entry, basic behavioral coding) are deeply appreciated and will typically earn you an official acknowledgement in posters, presentations, or publications.
* **Authorship:** Earning authorship on a scholarly presentation or publication requires substantial intellectual contribution to the project. This means going above and beyond routine tasks by actively contributing to the research design, statistical data analysis, or manuscript writing.

## 7. Mentorship & Letters of Recommendation

Students typically request letters of recommendation when applying to graduate school or professional positions so someone can vouch for their capability and character. 

**Policy:** We require a minimum commitment of **one full semester** in the lab before requesting a letter of recommendation.

**Guidance from Dr. Bonny:**
Dr. Bonny emphasizes that simply logging hours in the lab is not enough to secure a strong letter of recommendation. Recommendations are based on what is actively observed of you both inside and outside the classroom or lab environment. To receive a quality letter, you must take an active role in your own learning and professional development. This includes asking thoughtful questions during meetings, sharing research interests or relevant articles, and openly discussing your professional goals. By engaging deeply with the work and the team, you allow Dr. Bonny to get to know you as both a student and an individual, which is necessary to write a compelling and personalized letter on your behalf.
